The Basics

How Do You Figure Out What Something Is Worth?

The value of an item isn't always obvious. An old object isn't automatically valuable, and an item that looks ordinary can sometimes have a strong resale market.

01 — IDENTIFY

Find Out What It Is

Look for brands, model numbers, dates, signatures, labels, serial numbers, maker's marks, and other identifying details.

02 — INSPECT

Check the Condition

Look for damage, repairs, missing parts, wear, original packaging, and whether the item is complete and functional.

03 — RESEARCH

Compare Real Prices

Research similar items and, when possible, look at completed sales. An asking price doesn't necessarily mean that's what an item sold for.

Value Guides

Before you sell something, understanding the factors that affect value can help you make a more informed decision.

Condition Can Change Everything

Condition is one of the most important factors in resale value. Two identical items can sell for very different prices when one is complete and well preserved while the other has damage, missing parts, or extensive wear.

Examine the item carefully and document both its strengths and its defects before researching prices.

Find the Exact Model

A brand name alone may not identify an item precisely. Different models, production years, editions, sizes, and variations can have very different values.

Model numbers, date codes, labels, serial numbers, and maker's marks can help narrow down exactly what you have.

Don't Confuse Asking Prices With Sales

An item listed online for $500 isn't necessarily worth $500. The seller may never receive an offer at that price.

When possible, research completed sales or other evidence of actual transactions involving comparable items.

Older Doesn't Always Mean Valuable

Age is only one factor. Rarity, demand, condition, authenticity, brand, completeness, and collector interest can all affect value.

Some very old items are common, while newer or less obvious items can sometimes command much higher prices.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a photo tell me exactly what my item is worth?

Not necessarily. A photograph can provide useful clues, but authenticity, hidden damage, working condition, rarity, provenance, and other details may require additional research or physical inspection.

What should I photograph?

Photograph the entire item and include close-ups of labels, markings, signatures, model numbers, serial numbers, damage, unusual details, and other information that could help identify it.

How is an item's resale value determined?

Value can be influenced by condition, rarity, demand, brand, authenticity, completeness, age, provenance, and the marketplace where the item is sold.

Is an online estimate a professional appraisal?

No. Online estimates are informational and may not account for every factor considered during a professional appraisal. Rare, expensive, or potentially authenticated items may require evaluation by a qualified specialist.

Important:

What's It Worth? provides general informational estimates and educational resources. An estimated value is not a guarantee of what an item will sell for and should not be considered a professional appraisal.

Market prices can change, and actual selling prices may vary depending on condition, location, demand, authenticity, timing, and the marketplace used.
